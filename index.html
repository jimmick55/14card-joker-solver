How points are counted
  • Four players get 14 cards each from the 56-card deck. Each player discards one card, which is out of play, and sets the other 13 as front (3), middle (5) and back (5). The solver picks your discard.
  • Each row you win against an opponent pays 1 point plus that row's royalty. Each row you lose costs 1 point plus the opponent's royalty. Royalties are paid only when the row wins.
  • Winning all three rows against one opponent doubles everything won from that opponent, royalties included. Losing all three doubles what you pay.
  • Scooping all three opponents doubles again what each one pays you (×4 in total). The same applies against you: an opponent who scoops you and both other players collects ×4 from you.
  • Royalties. Front: trips 2, two jokers 19, three jokers 29 (each in place of the trips 2). Middle: full house 1, quads 7, straight flush 9, five of a kind 19. Back: quads 3, straight flush 4, five of a kind 9.
  • Two jokers in the front make trips of the third card (two jokers with a 5 are three 5s). Three jokers are three Aces. Either way, the middle must be a straight or better.
  • There are no naturals and no face-up cards, so nothing doubles the stakes.
  • Straights rank A-K-Q-J-10, then A-2-3-4-5, then K-high down to 6-high. Ties go to the suit of the highest card (♠ ♥ ♣ ♦). A joker that decides a suit tiebreak counts as a spade. In a flush, jokers become the highest missing ranks.
  • Opponents choose their discard and set their hands with a strength model that weighs each row's chance of winning and royalties, tuned on tens of thousands of simulated 14-card hands. They never foul. Real opponents may play better or worse, so treat the totals as estimates.
  • "Per row" values include the scoop doubling, so front + middle + back = total. Every setup is scored against the same simulated deals, so the ranking between setups is sharper than the ± range suggests.
